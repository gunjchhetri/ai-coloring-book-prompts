## 1. Nutmeg the kitchen mouse decorates

**Ages:** 3-6 · **Pages:** 24 · **Size:** 8.5 × 11 in · **Style:** Story · **Quality:** Standard · **Credits:** 48 Standard · 72 Premium

```text
A 24-page Christmas story. Nutmeg, a tiny brown mouse in a red knitted hat, lives behind the Bakers' kitchen wall. While the family sleeps in December she decorates in secret: a cranberry string along the shelf, a thimble bell, a gingerbread cottage built from crumbs, a walnut wrapped as a present and a stocking sewn from a sock. She rides the toy train round the tree, slides down the banister on a spoon and hides from the cat in a slipper. On Christmas morning the Baker children find her tiny stocking and leave a crumb of cake inside.
```

2. Nutmeg in her cozy room inside the wall pulling on her knitted hat, a matchbox bed, a thimble cup and a round button rug around her.  
   *Words:* Nutmeg had a matchbox bed, a button rug and a red knitted hat that kept her ears warm.

   *Words:* She found a bowl of cranberries on the counter and rolled out one, two, three.

   *Words:* She threaded them on a long string and hung them along the shelf like a necklace.

   *Words:* She hung a shiny thimble from a hook. Ting! It was her very own Christmas bell.
8. Nutmeg gathering gingerbread crumbs into a pile beside a cooling rack of gingerbread cookies shaped like stars and little people.  
   *Words:* Beside the cookie rack lay crumbs of gingerbread. Nutmeg had a wonderful idea.
9. Nutmeg patting the roof of a tiny gingerbread cottage she built from crumbs, frosting dots along the roof and a chocolate chip for a door.  
   *Words:* She built a little gingerbread cottage with a frosting roof and a chocolate chip door.

   *Words:* From an old lost sock, she sewed a little stocking, stitch by tiny stitch.

   *Words:* Then Nutmeg tiptoed into the living room. The tree was so tall and sparkly!

   *Words:* She hopped onto the toy train. Choo choo! Around and around the tree she went.

   *Words:* She sat in a spoon and slid all the way down the banister. Wheee!
15. Nutmeg landing on her bottom at the foot of the stairs, the spoon beside her, while the tabby cat on the rug opens one big eye.  
   *Words:* Bump! She landed at the bottom of the stairs. The cat opened one eye.
16. Nutmeg hiding inside a big fluffy slipper with only her ear tips showing, the tabby cat sniffing the slipper with its whiskers twitching.  
   *Words:* Nutmeg dived into a fluffy slipper and kept very, very still.
17. The tabby cat flopping back to sleep on the hearth rug with a huge yawn, Nutmeg peeking out of the slipper with a relieved smile, three stockings on the mantel above.  
   *Words:* The cat yawned and went back to sleep. Phew! Nutmeg crept out of the slipper.
18. Nutmeg on Christmas Eve hanging her tiny sock stocking on a nail beside her mouse hole, the gingerbread cottage and walnut present beside it.  
   *Words:* On Christmas Eve, she hung her little stocking by her mouse hole and put the walnut present beside it.

   *Words:* Then she climbed into her matchbox bed and fell fast asleep.
20. Christmas morning: the Baker girl and boy in pajamas stopping in the kitchen doorway, pointing at the cranberry garland and the thimble bell.  
   *Words:* On Christmas morning, the Baker children ran into the kitchen. "Look! Who did all this?"
21. The Baker girl kneeling on the floor by the mouse hole, finding the tiny stocking, the gingerbread cottage and the walnut tied with a bow.  
   *Words:* By the mouse hole they found a tiny stocking, a gingerbread cottage and a walnut wrapped with a bow.
22. The Baker boy dropping a crumb of Christmas cake into the tiny stocking while his sister holds a plate with the rest of the cake.  
   *Words:* "A mouse made Christmas for us," said the little boy. They put a crumb of cake in the stocking.
23. Nutmeg peeking out of her mouse hole and hugging the crumb of cake she found in her stocking, a big smile on her face.  
   *Words:* When Nutmeg peeked out, she found the cake in her stocking. She hugged it tight.
24. The warm kitchen: the Baker children lying on their tummies waving at the mouse hole, Nutmeg waving back, the cat asleep in its basket.  
   *Words:* "Merry Christmas," whispered Nutmeg, and the Baker children waved back.

## 2. Thistle the reindeer trains for the sleigh

**Ages:** 6-9 · **Pages:** 24 · **Size:** 8.5 × 11 in · **Style:** Story · **Quality:** Standard · **Credits:** 48 Standard · 72 Premium

```text
A 24-page Christmas story. Thistle, a young reindeer with one crooked antler and a jingle collar, joins the North Pole flight school to earn a place on the sleigh team. Training brings take-off practice on the ice runway, a crash into the snowman orchard, loop-the-loops over the elf village, night flying by lantern, rooftop landings on the workshop and a harness relay. She keeps finishing last. On Christmas Eve a blizzard grounds the team, but her crooked antler catches the wind just right and she leads the sleigh through. It ends with a banner stitched over her stall.
```

Paste one page at a time into ChatGPT, Gemini or any AI image tool, with this line after it:

> Black-and-white story coloring page, age 6 to 9. Thistle: a slim young reindeer, one straight antler and one bent sideways, a jingle-bell collar, a white chest patch. Coach Birch: a big old reindeer with a whistle. Elves in pointed hats. Bold lines, medium detail, simple snowy background, no shading or grey, no words in the picture, blank band at the bottom for text, portrait 8.5 x 11 page.

1. Thistle the young reindeer standing in a snowy field at the edge of the North Pole village, gazing up as the sleigh team flies over the rooftops.  
   *Words:* Thistle was a young reindeer with one crooked antler, and she dreamed of pulling the Christmas sleigh.
2. A line of young reindeer waiting at the wooden gate of the flight school, bells hanging from the arch, an elf with a clipboard calling the next one.  
   *Words:* In November she lined up at the North Pole Flight School with all the other young reindeer.
3. Coach Birch, a big old reindeer with a whistle on a cord, leaning down to frown at Thistle's crooked antler while the other young reindeer stare.  
   *Words:* Coach Birch looked at her bent antler. "Hmm," he said. "We'll see what you can do."
4. Take-off practice on an ice runway lined with lanterns, young reindeer galloping toward the sky while Thistle slips and spins with her legs splayed.  
   *Words:* First came take-off practice on the ice runway. Thistle slipped and slid and spun around.
5. Thistle finally in the air above the runway, tipping sideways because of her crooked antler, the other reindeer flying straight ahead.  
   *Words:* When she finally lifted off, her crooked antler tipped her sideways.
6. Thistle crash-landed in an orchard of snowmen, her back legs sticking out of a snowdrift and a snowman's head rolling away.  
   *Words:* Crash! She landed in the snowman orchard and knocked the head off a snowman.
7. Three elves in pointed hats laughing kindly as they lift the snowman's head back on, Thistle shaking snow off her ears beside them.  
   *Words:* The elves put the snowman's head back on. "Try again tomorrow," they told her.
8. Thistle practising alone at sunrise, leaping off a tall snowbank with a determined face, the sun peeking over the ice hills.  
   *Words:* So Thistle practised every morning before anyone else was awake.
9. Loop-the-loop class over the elf village: older reindeer flying perfect circles above smoking chimneys while Thistle flies a wobbly squiggle.  
   *Words:* In loop class the big reindeer flew perfect circles over the elf village. Thistle flew a wobbly squiggle.
10. Thistle dangling upside down, tangled in a string of Christmas lights strung between two elf cottages, an elf peering up from a doorway.  
   *Words:* She ended up tangled in a string of Christmas lights between two rooftops.
11. Night flying: a line of reindeer crossing a starry sky, each with a lantern hanging from its harness, the crescent moon above.  
   *Words:* Then came night flying. Each reindeer carried a lantern through the dark sky.
12. Thistle alone behind a big cloud, her lantern swinging, the lights of the other reindeer disappearing far ahead.  
   *Words:* Thistle's lantern swung, and she lost the line behind a cloud.
13. A snowy owl flying beside Thistle in the night sky, pointing a wing toward the glowing windows of the toy workshop far below.  
   *Words:* A snowy owl flew beside her. "Follow the workshop lights," it hooted, and she found her way back.
14. Rooftop landing practice: Thistle skidding across the steep snowy roof of the toy workshop and bumping into its big brick chimney.  
   *Words:* At rooftop landing practice, Thistle skidded across the workshop roof and bumped the chimney.
15. A harness relay across a snowy field, older reindeer pulling small sleds past flag poles, Thistle far behind as elves cheer from a fence.  
   *Words:* In the harness relay against the older reindeer, Thistle pulled hard, but she finished last again.
16. Thistle lying sadly in her barn stall at night with her chin on a pile of hay, a row of stalls with hanging blankets beside hers.  
   *Words:* That night Thistle lay in the barn. "Maybe a crooked antler can't pull a sleigh," she sighed.
17. Grandmother Juniper, the white-haired sleigh driver in a long coat and fur hat, holding a lantern and patting Thistle's head over the stall door.  
   *Words:* The old sleigh driver, Grandmother Juniper, came by with a lantern. "Don't give up yet, little one," she said.
18. Christmas Eve: the big sleigh piled high with sacks, the team in harness, a howling blizzard swirling as elves hold on to their hats.  
   *Words:* On Christmas Eve the sleigh was packed, but a howling blizzard swept over the North Pole.
19. The big reindeer pushed back down by the wind, legs braced in the snow, Coach Birch shaking his head beside the sleigh.  
   *Words:* The big reindeer tried to fly, but the wind pushed them back down. "We're grounded," said Coach Birch.
20. Thistle stepping alone into the blizzard, snow streaming around her crooked antler like wind round a sail, her hooves lifting off the ground.  
   *Words:* Thistle stepped into the storm. Her crooked antler caught the wind just right, and she rose straight up.
21. Thistle harnessed at the very front of the reindeer team, leading the sleigh up through swirling snow, Grandmother Juniper holding the reins.  
   *Words:* "Put her at the front!" cried Grandmother Juniper. Thistle led the team up through the snow.
22. The sleigh flying above the clouds under a calm, starry sky, the rooftops and chimneys of a small town peeking through the clouds below.  
   *Words:* Above the clouds the sky was calm and full of stars. They delivered every present before dawn.
23. The sleigh landing back at the North Pole at dawn, elves throwing their hats in the air and Coach Birch giving Thistle a proud nod.  
   *Words:* When they landed back home, everyone cheered. Coach Birch gave Thistle a big nod.
24. Thistle standing proudly in her stall under a new stitched banner decorated with one crooked antler, a wreath on the stall door.  
   *Words:* The next morning a new banner hung over Thistle's stall, stitched with her name and one crooked antler.

## 3. Wilbur the penguin and the lost letter

**Ages:** 6-9 · **Pages:** 24 · **Size:** 8.5 × 11 in · **Style:** Story · **Quality:** Standard · **Credits:** 48 Standard · 72 Premium

```text
A 24-page Christmas story. Wilbur, a penguin postmaster in a blue cap, runs the North Pole Post Office. In December letters pour in by plane, boat and sled; he sorts them, stamps them and sends them to the workshop. On December 23 he finds one letter behind a mailbag, from a girl named Hana asking for her grandpa's lost music box. Wilbur sets off by dog sled, crosses an iced-over lake, borrows a lantern from a lighthouse keeper and finds the music box at a toy-repair shop. He reaches the workshop as the sleigh lifts off, and on Christmas morning Hana's grandpa hears his song again.
```
